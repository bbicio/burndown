Getting Started

PDash is a portfolio-management tool for consulting teams: track commercial offers through a sales pipeline, plan who's working on what, and monitor budget consumption against actuals. Everything below assumes you already have an account — invites are sent by an administrator (§16.2).

Signing in

Enter your email and password on the login page. A session lasts 8 hours before you're signed out automatically — there's no warning as it approaches, your next click simply returns you to the login page.

Details

Wrong password and an unrecognized email produce the exact same generic error, with no hint about which one was wrong. This is deliberate: it stops anyone from using the login form to discover which email addresses have accounts. A disabled account is refused even with the correct password.

Forgot your password?

Use "Forgot password" on the login page. You'll always see the same success message, whether or not that email has an account — if it does, a reset link arrives by email, valid for 2 hours.

Details

The identical always-succeeds response is the same anti-enumeration protection as login: it prevents anyone from using the reset form to check which emails are registered. Every password you set — via reset, via the initial invite link, or via Change Password in your account menu — must be at least 8 characters.

Accepting Terms & Conditions

The first time you log in, and again any time an administrator publishes a new version, you're taken to an acceptance page before you can continue. Tick the box to enable the continue button; you're then returned to wherever you were headed.

Pipeline

Every commercial offer lives here as a card on a kanban board, organized into six stages. This is where offers are built, priced, shared, and eventually turned into real projects.

Deal stages

StageMeaning
DraftPrivate working copy — only its creator can see it; excluded from every column total and filter.
SIPStrategic intent / early prospect.
ExpectedQualified opportunity, likely to close.
AnticipatedHigh confidence, close imminent.
CommittedDeal signed — committed revenue.
CanceledOpportunity withdrawn or lost.

Each column (except Draft) has a running total in its footer.

Details

If every offer in a column shares one currency, the footer is a single plain total. If a column mixes currencies, the footer instead shows one subtotal per currency (with a small EUR-equivalent next to each non-EUR line), followed by a bold combined total — the sum of every offer's fee converted to EUR at that offer's own exchange rate, never a naive sum across currencies. Pass-through costs follow the same pattern as a secondary line under each subtotal.

Offer cards and the detail panel

Each card shows the offer name, stage badge, total budget, phase/task counts, and any linked project(s). Clicking anywhere on a card except its action buttons opens a right-side detail panel.

Detail panel contents

Details

The header carries Delete (Draft stage only), Clone, Share, Edit, and Close, plus a row of version tabs above the panel body — always visible, even for a single-version offer. Deleting a proposal's only remaining version deletes the whole proposal, since every proposal always has at least one.

Left column: offer name and stage badge, version label and creation date, dates, currency, rate card name (if set), notes, the total budget split into Fee + Pass-Through Costs, a JSON export button, and the linked-projects list — each entry shows the project's ID, name, status badge, any assigned task names, and a "📊 Project Dashboard" button (visible only once that project has timesheet data).

Right column: the full phase → task → role breakdown, with hours and budget rolling up from role to task to phase to a grand total.

Viewer permission hides Clone, Share, and Edit entirely from this panel (see §18).

Filtering the board

A filter bar between the title and the columns lets you narrow what's visible without changing what you're allowed to see — search by offer or client name, then filter by Owner, Client, Currency, or deal Value.

Details

Value buckets: €0–20K, €20K–50K, €50K–100K, €100K–200K, €200K+, with an "Include PTC" toggle deciding whether pass-through costs count toward the bucket. Selecting several values within one filter is an OR (e.g. two owners at once); different filters combine with AND. Filters never hide an offer you're otherwise allowed to see — they only narrow the already-visible set — and the Draft column is always shown in full regardless of any filter, since it's private to begin with. A "Clear filters" link appears once anything is active, and every filter resets when the page reloads.

Board toolbar

  • "+ New Proposal" opens a blank offer in the editor (hidden for non-admins on years marked inactive).

Publishing a Draft to SIP

A one-way move — once published, a version can never go back to Draft. It's also the moment the offer becomes visible to anyone else on the team.

If the same offer has other Draft versions besides the one you're publishing, they are permanently deleted as part of publishing. The confirmation dialog names them before this happens.

Cloning an offer

Clone always creates a brand-new, separate offer — never a new version of the one you're viewing. The clone's first version is always labeled "v1," regardless of the source version's own label.

Structure and cost calculation

An offer is organized as Phases → Tasks → Roles. Each task-role cell holds an hours figure. Task cost = Σ(hours × role's effective rate) plus that task's own pass-through costs; phase cost sums its tasks; the offer total sums its phases.

Details — the task-cost walkthrough
  1. The unit is hours, not days. Each task-role cell is an hours figure, entered directly.
  2. Task cost = Σ(hours × that role's effective rate) across every role on the task, plus that task's own pass-through costs.
  3. Phase cost = the sum of its tasks' costs; offer total = the sum of its phases' costs — pure sums at both levels, nothing re-derived or re-rated.
  4. The bridge to a project. When a task migrates into a project, each role's hours figure carries over unchanged as that role's Sold Hours on the new project task — a direct copy, no recalculation.
  5. The bridge to Resource Planning. Resource Planning's "Sold" column is that exact same figure, summed however the active view groups it — a number you see there traces back, unchanged, to what was typed into the offer editor.

The effective rate for a role follows a fallback chain: a client's own rate-card override (§7.2, highest priority) → that role's per-currency agency default (§7.6) → the EUR rate converted at the current exchange factor (§7.7, lowest-priority fallback).

Role columns

Each role column carries four controls: move it left/right, change the role (keeping its position and hours), duplicate it under a different role, or remove it outright. The compact-header toggle hides all four to save space.

Locking

A version becomes read-only (🔒) once either: the offer is Committed and every task has already been migrated to a project, or another version of the same offer already has a linked project.

Details

Being Committed does not by itself lock the editor — while any task remains unmapped to a project, the version stays fully editable and "Generate Project" stays available for the rest. An offer can generate more than one project over time.

Generating a project from an offer

Once an offer is ready, its tasks migrate into real projects — either all at once, or in stages.

Details — the three real scenarios
  1. Selecting every remaining task at once. One project is created immediately, no program step, no prompts.
  2. Selecting only some of the tasks. If the offer has no program yet, a "Create program" step appears — create a new one, or link to an existing program. Canceling this step aborts the whole generation with nothing created. Once a program is established this way, every later generation from the same offer — partial or the remaining full set — auto-links to it with no further prompt. Any editor of the offer can do this, not just admins.
  3. Adding tasks to an already-linked project instead of creating a new one. The same selection toolbar also offers a dropdown of the offer's existing linked project(s) plus an "＋ Add to project" button once at least one exists. A confirmation step lists exactly which tasks will be added; the dropdown always resets after use.
If the underlying save fails to reach the server mid-generation, you'll see an explicit sync-failure warning rather than a false success message.

Saving

Every field edit in the editor autosaves in the background, confirmed by a brief toast — there's nothing to click for an edit to persist. A Save button is also available for an explicit, immediate save.

Resource Planning

A cross-project view of sold hours against actuals — grouped by role, by project, or by owner — and what still needs to be planned into the future.

What a row means, per view

ViewRow structure
By RoleOne row per role, expandable (collapsed by default) into one child row per project/task that role covers.
By ProjectProject → Task → Role → Owner — the most granular view.
By OwnerOwner → Project → Task — deliberately coarser: every role a person logged time under on a task is combined into that one row, because the point of this view is "what is this person working on," not their role.

Reading the columns

Time-period columns (months or weeks) across the date range you've selected, plus three summary columns on every row: Sold, From actuals, To be planned.

How the numbers are built

Details — the full walkthrough

Sold is the estimated hours from the offer/project's own task-role data, summed to match the row and the active Team filter.

From actuals (past periods only) is matching timesheet hours, matched to the row by task name and role.

Residual, and the one genuinely counter-intuitive rule: Residual = max(0, Sold − From actuals), computed per individual task/role, never at the row's total level, then floored at zero. This is why "To be planned" can sometimes look bigger than "Sold minus From actuals" suggests: if one task among several is already over-consumed, its own shortfall floors to zero and stops contributing there — it does not pull down other, still-healthy tasks' remaining residual. This is intentional, documented behavior, not a bug — the app itself carries a tooltip explaining it.

To be planned spreads that residual across future weeks: if the task's own month-by-month distribution sums to roughly 100%, the residual follows that distribution; otherwise it splits evenly across the task's own remaining weeks (counted from the task's own dates, not whatever's currently visible on screen, so the number doesn't change as you page through the calendar).

Monthly pulse — only in the even-split case, and only when that would put less than 1 hour/week on a task — shows the whole month's share once, on that month's first week, instead of a tiny fraction repeated every week.

Rounded is purely cosmetic — whole numbers vs. two decimals. It never changes any total or export.

Gantt view

A separate, task-level bar chart per project. It uses simpler math than the table above: bars show consumption as a percentage of sold hours, not the sold-minus-consumed residual — the two views intentionally answer different questions.

Details

A bar's color reflects task status, not pipeline stage: grey if the task is non-billable, green if completed, red if actual hours exceed sold hours, blue otherwise. Today's week is always highlighted.

Overallocation color coding

A row's load shows in red once it exceeds 30 hours/week. This is a fixed display rule, not an analysis — there is no AI-driven overallocation feature (see §11's note on this).

KPI cards

Six figures: Total Sold Hours, Total Budget, Hours Consumed, Budget Consumed, Hours Left, Budget Left — computed live from the project's own task and actuals data.

This is a different calculation path from the portfolio overview's own summary table (which reads the manually-maintained Phasing grid, §7.1) — the two can disagree if Phasing hasn't been kept in sync with task edits.

Burndown chart

A line chart, filterable to one task or the whole project, monthly or weekly. Its solid line is actual consumption; up to two dashed reference lines show what was planned (from Phasing and/or from the Planning grid), so you can see at a glance whether spend is running ahead of or behind plan.

Monthly Summary Table

One row per month, split into Hours (Estimated/Consumed/Variance) and Budget (Estimated/Spent/Variance), plus a Pass-Through-Costs column when the project has any.

Details

Variance highlighting only flags the bad case: a negative (over-budget/over-hours) variance shows bold red; a positive variance has no special color.

Summary by Task / Role / Functional Area

Three pivot cards below the monthly table, breaking the same actuals data down three different ways.

Details

Summary by task — one column per task, regardless of role.

Summary by role — one column per (role, task) pair actually present, not per role alone. A role billed at different rates on different tasks (for example, an "Account Director" priced at 168/h on one task and 130/h on another) gets two separate, internally-consistent columns instead of one blended one.

Summary by functional area — one column per functional group (§7.1), where each group's membership is a list of specific (role, task) pairs — or a role claimed for "any task." This lets a group claim a role's hours on one task while excluding that same role's hours on a different task billed at a different rate.

Configuration

Admin-only setup screens, reached from the Configuration page: project-level fields, plus registries for clients, client groups, programs, roles, pipeline years/targets, and currencies.

7.1 Project Configuration

One full-page form per project: identity fields, tasks, phasing, planning, functional groups, and actuals management.

FieldNotes
NameMust match the D365 Project Name in the timesheet export
Start / End dateMonth-level
Currency, Pipeline, Status, Client, ProgramStatus's allowed values depend on Pipeline stage

Monthly % distribution

Shown only for a task spanning more than one month — one editable percentage cell per covered month, with a live badge showing whether the row currently sums to 100%. Cells lock once a task is Completed, is single-month, or you're a viewer.

Phasing and Planning grids

Both are plain, freely-editable input grids by default — one currency/hours cell per month. Two bulk-fill actions can populate them instead of hand-entry.

Details — Derive from Task Dates vs. Reforecast

Derive from Task Dates is always available and uses only each task's own dates and sold hours/budget — no actuals. It spreads every task's total across the months it overlaps, by day-overlap fraction, touching both past and future months equally.

Reforecast only appears once actuals exist for the project. Past months are overwritten with real spend and hours from the uploaded timesheets (scaled down if actuals exceed the sold total, so a task never shows over 100% consumed). Future months redistribute the remaining budget/hours — following the task's monthly distribution if it sums to ~100%, otherwise split evenly across the remaining months.

Neither action has an undo. A run only updates the on-screen grids — nothing is saved to the server until you click the page's own Save button — and there's no unsaved-changes warning if you navigate away first; the values are simply lost.

If Reforecast's carried-forward drift would push a month above 100%, it stops entirely rather than partially applying — no task after the offending one is touched, and an inline message names the task and the month. You adjust that task's monthly distribution manually before retrying.

Pass Through Costs

External costs not tied to sold hours — licenses, travel, and similar — added as individual line items with a title, optional note, amount, and month. A running total sums every item; any item can be removed.

Functional Groups

Each group is a name plus a list of role rows — a role name (must match the timesheet's role column) plus a task dropdown, defaulting to "any task." Picking a specific task claims that role's hours only on that task for this group.

Actuals section

Load Actuals, View (a popup listing every imported row — visible to viewers too), Download actuals (an Excel file named by client/project/code/date), and Delete actuals (hidden for viewers, permanent).

7.2 Clients

A simple name registry, used to group projects. Each client row has a 💲 button opening a rate-card modal — Agency default (read-only) and Client custom (blank = use the default) per role, plus one column per active non-EUR currency. Rate-card management lives only here.

7.3 Client Groups

Named bundles of clients, used as a revenue-target scope when several clients share one goal. A client belongs to at most one group; deleting a group only ungroups its clients, it doesn't delete them.

7.4 Pipelines & POTs

What a POT is

A Client POT is the total target revenue — the maximum "wallet potential" — allocated to a specific client (or client group) for one forecasting year: existing recurring business plus identified upsell/cross-sell opportunity, combined into one benchmark.

Setting one up

"+ New POT" targets a client, a client group, or one of two general scopes ("Unassigned / To be Identified", "New Biz") — amount and year only.

Tracking progress

A banner shows the total target and achievement % across every POT for the year; five stage-summary cards show count and fee value per pipeline stage; a "🔍 View Details" modal per POT shows its own KPI cards, a change history, and the list of matching proposals. The same progress figure is also visible from the Pipeline board's detail panel for a linked offer.

7.5 Programs

A simple ID + name registry, used to group projects across the portfolio.

A program's ID is permanently fixed once created — there's no way to correct a typo later, only to create a new program and migrate its projects.
Deleting a program is blocked outright while any project is still linked to it — the delete does not proceed. The confirmation dialog's wording currently suggests projects get unlinked instead; that text doesn't match this actual (blocking) behavior — it's a known, tracked issue with the dialog text itself, not with what actually happens.

7.6 Roles Registry

Label, code (must match the timesheet's role code), team (auto-derived from the code), default hourly rate, and one optional "Default rate" field per active non-EUR currency.

Details

Leaving a currency's default rate blank means "convert the EUR rate at the current exchange rate"; a value fixes this role's own default in that currency regardless of later exchange-rate movement. This is the middle link in the rate fallback chain (§4): a client's rate-card override takes priority over it, and it in turn takes priority over the plain EUR-rate-times-exchange-factor fallback.

7.7 Currencies

EUR is fixed (1:1, always active, not editable). Any other currency starts inactive — it only becomes selectable elsewhere once an admin activates it here with an initial "1 EUR = X" rate.

Details

An active currency's rate can be updated any time, each change timestamped; a History button shows the full rate log for that currency (not available for EUR, whose rate never changes). This is the one exchange rate the whole app reads for every cross-currency figure — the Pipeline board's mixed-currency totals, a client's rate card, and any other cross-currency aggregation all use this same admin-managed rate.

Timesheet Upload

How actual hours make it into PDash, and how to review or clean up what's been imported.

Where you upload from

Two entry points that do the same thing: a project's own configuration form, and its Project Reporting detail page — both have a "📂 Load Actuals" button.

Expected columns
ColumnNotes
DateSeveral formats accepted; day/month order disambiguated automatically

Owner: NamePerson who logged the hours
HoursDecimal, comma or period accepted
Task/IssueMust match a task name in the project's configuration

Replace, not append

Uploading a new file for a project code replaces every previously-imported row for that code — it does not add to them. Rows with a missing project ID are skipped entirely.

The rate snapshot

Each row's hourly rate is resolved and locked in once, at import time. A later rate change doesn't retroactively affect already-imported rows — re-uploading is the only way to pick up a new rate.

Date parsing

Unambiguous whenever one date number is over 12; falls back to a default order only when genuinely ambiguous. A date that isn't a real calendar date rejects the entire upload, naming the offending row — no partial import.

Reviewing what was uploaded (admin only)

The Timesheet Management page lists every project code with uploaded data — Client, Project, Project code, Uploads, Rows, Last uploaded — sortable and filterable, with a pipeline-year selector (default: current year if active, else the most recent active year, plus an explicit "All years").

AI Sidebar

Opened from the "🤖 AI Chat" button in the top-right of the navbar. Requires an API key configured in Settings (§9).

Planning Assistant

A chat interface with context automatically built from project config, task breakdown, role assignments, and monthly allocation — ask free-form planning questions and get forward-looking estimates.

Project Analysis

Triggered per project from Project Reporting. Returns a RAG status with rationale, burn-rate analysis, budget-risk assessment, planning variance, task-level notes, and recommendations.

Where the request actually goes

AI calls go directly from your browser to the provider you chose (Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google) — never through PDash's own servers. Your API key and the full request, which for Project Analysis includes real financial figures, are sent client-side straight to the provider. PDash's servers never see the prompt or the response.

There is no "Resource Allocation Analysis" AI feature — an earlier draft of this manual's source material described one, but it does not exist in the app. The only overallocation-related behavior is the plain, non-AI color rule described under Resource Planning (§5).

User Administration

Reached via the User Admin page — admin or sysadmin only. Three account tiers govern what's possible.

RoleDescription
sysadminEverything admin has, plus exclusive access to DB Reset and Terms & Conditions editing
adminFull access to all data and configuration
userScoped access — owns and sees only their own resources (plus anything shared with them)

No account starts as sysadmin — the first one is set up outside the product; every promotion after that goes through the toggle below.

Changing a user's role or status

Any admin/sysadmin can toggle a user between admin and user. Granting or revoking sysadmin is sysadmin-only, and is always two-step: user → admin then admin → sysadmin (never a direct jump); demotion mirrors it. No one can change their own role or status — your own row shows "(you)" instead of action buttons. A sysadmin's row can only be modified by another sysadmin; a plain admin sees no action buttons there at all.

Anonymizing a user

Available only on a disabled, not-yet-anonymized account, behind an explicit confirmation. Replaces their name and email with anonymized placeholders while preserving their operational records (cost grids, projects). No one can anonymize their own account, and a plain admin can't anonymize a sysadmin.

Terms & Conditions editor

Sysadmin-exclusive. Edit the current draft's HTML, "Save draft" without publishing (users are unaffected), or "Publish new version" — which permanently records the draft as a new version and forces everyone to re-accept on their next login. A "👁 Preview" button opens the live acceptance page in a new tab so you can check the draft's appearance first.

Details

Every published version is retained permanently — publishing a new one never erases the previous text. A Version History list shows every past version with its publish date and publisher; clicking one opens its full text, read-only.

DB Reset

Sysadmin-exclusive hidden page for bulk, irreversible deletions. Every destructive action here requires typing the literal word DELETE into a confirmation field — not just clicking through a dialog.

GDPR & Data Rights

Terms & Conditions gate

If you've never accepted the current version — or a newer one was published since your last acceptance — you're redirected to an acceptance page before continuing.

Your own data

"My Profile," from the account menu, lets you update your own first name, last name, and email at any time.

Right to erasure

Handled via the anonymize action (§16) — admin-performed, not self-service, and only available once an account has been disabled.

Sharing & Permissions

Every offer and project has an owner and can be shared with specific colleagues at Editor or Viewer level.

Ownership

Whoever creates an offer or project is its owner by default. Disabling a user doesn't remove their ownership — an admin can reassign it.

Details

From the offer editor, any admin/sysadmin can reassign a proposal's owner directly, regardless of the version's lock state. This also grants the new owner Editor access to every project already linked to that proposal (without downgrading an existing owner of one of those projects) and emails them a notification listing what they gained.

Sharing an offer or project

Search for an existing platform user by name or email — only real accounts can be granted access, no free-text invites — and grant Editor or Viewer. Sharing is not available at all on a Draft offer, since Draft is private to its creator by definition.

Sharing a whole program

A program group's header has its own "🔗 Share Program" button — granting the chosen permission on every project in that program in one action, rather than one at a time. Only an admin, or someone who already owns/edits at least one project in the program, can do this.

Seeing who has access

Both the pipeline board's detail panel and the full-page offer editor show a "Shared with" list without opening the Share modal — owner name, creation date, and every share with its permission badge. A non-owner share can be removed directly from this list; adding a new share or changing a permission still needs the Share modal.
